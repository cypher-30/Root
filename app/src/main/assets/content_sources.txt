Root content provenance

Every starter phrase is a short everyday expression checked against the public
reference named below. This is a small starter selection for each language,
not an imported course, and not a claim to cover every dialect, register, or
respectful form of address. English prompts say "one person" / "more than one
person" where a source distinguishes singular and plural.

Status for everything below: source-checked; native-speaker review for this
app is pending. No source illustrations or course materials have been
imported. Six starter phrases play a real recording (listed under "Reference
recordings" below); every other phrase and every lesson has no audio rather
than a synthetic voice.

Dholuo (39 phrases: Greetings, Family, Market, Numbers, Food, Meeting people)
Wikivoyage contributors, "Luo phrasebook", CC BY-SA 4.0
https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Luo_phrasebook
Spelling is normalized to the "dh" spelling used elsewhere in Root (the
phrasebook writes, for example, "Ithi nade?" for "Idhi nade?"). The three
original greetings (Amosi, Idhi nade?, Erokamano) were hand-entered earlier
and are kept for existing learners' history.

Shona (40 phrases: Greetings, Meeting people, Food & market, Getting around,
Numbers & time)
Omniglot, "Useful Shona phrases", phrases contributed by Emma Thembani and
Ernest Mdende; single food words checked against Wiktionary (CC BY-SA 4.0);
numbers checked against Omniglot, "Numbers in Shona".
https://www.omniglot.com/language/phrases/shona.php
https://www.omniglot.com/language/numbers/shona.htm
Omniglot content was checked for accuracy only; it is not license-cleared
for redistribution beyond these short phrase pairs.

Swahili (40 phrases: Greetings, Meeting people, Food & market, Getting
around, Numbers & time)
Omniglot, "Useful Swahili phrases" (credits Ylanne Sorrows, Rushomesa
Remigius, Dwayne Panzeter, Corona Cermak, and EasySwahili), and Wikivoyage
contributors, "Swahili phrasebook", CC BY-SA 4.0.
https://www.omniglot.com/language/phrases/swahili.php
https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Swahili_phrasebook
Swahili has many regional varieties (Kenya, Tanzania, DRC and more); these
are the sources' standard/coastal forms only.

Amharic (40 phrases: Greetings, Directions, Meeting people, Food & market,
Numbers & time)
FSI Amharic Basic Course, Units 1-2 (Basic Sentences), Serge Obolensky,
Debebow Zelelie, Mulugeta Andualem, Foreign Service Institute, U.S.
Department of State, 1964. A work of the U.S. federal government, with no
U.S. copyright (17 U.S.C. Section 105). Its romanization is kept exactly as
printed (capital K for an ejective consonant, ñ/š for palatal sounds), not
Ge'ez script or IPA.
Informal greetings, meeting people, food, and numbers were checked against
Omniglot, "Useful Amharic phrases", and Wikivoyage contributors, "Amharic
phrasebook" (CC BY-SA 4.0), and are shown with Ge'ez script in brackets.
https://www.omniglot.com/language/phrases/amharic.php
https://en.wikivoyage.org/wiki/Amharic_phrasebook
The learner's other Amharic references (dictionaries, grammars, courses,
scripture translations) are separately copyrighted and were deliberately not
used.

Learn units
Each lesson in Learn is written only with the starter phrases above (and, for
Shona greetings, the Shona pilot unit's own source-checked lines). English
explanations were written for Root. Listening steps say plainly that no real
recording exists yet; they are never filled with synthetic speech.
